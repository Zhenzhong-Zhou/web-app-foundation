# Trace a lot

A lot is a quantity with its own code and expiry, received from a supplier or made in a production run. Tracing a lot shows where it is, where it came from, what it went into and who received it: the list a recall has to reach.

## Trace a lot {#trace}

1. Open **Trace a lot**.
2. Type any part of the lot code, or of the supplier’s lot kept beside a code made here; the batch number is usually enough. Press Enter.
3. Choose the lot from those listed. Its whole code opens it at once.

You can also find a lot by its code or its supplier’s lot in **Search everything**: its row gives its item’s code, then when it expires or **Expiry to add**, then its supplier’s lot or **Supplier’s lot to add**. Or press a lot’s code wherever one is listed: on Inventory, under **Where it stands** on an item’s page, and under **Waiting for a cost** on Stock value. **Trace lot** on a row of Inventory opens it too.

## Read a trace {#read}

Under the lot’s code, its item’s name opens the item’s page, for someone who may see items, and **All movements of this lot** opens Movements on its item with this lot alone, over its whole life, with no dates.

- **Where it is now**: each location holding some, written by its whole path from its warehouse, and how much. Someone who may not see locations reads how much is **Usable** and **Not usable** instead, with no place named. Where part of the lot was moved into another lot, **Moved on to** lists each such lot, however many moves on, with all it holds now: a recall reaches that stock too.
- **Where it came from**: its receipt and the supplier’s order, or the run that made it and its licence. A receipt with no order says whom it came from, where it was given, and why, such as “Received with no order: opening stock”; stock moved in from another lot names that lot; and the lot made when lot tracking started reads “Stock on hand when lot tracking started”.
- **Made from**: the component lots a run used, however many steps back.
- **Went into**: the runs that used it, and the lots they made.
- **Who received it**: every customer this lot, or anything made from it, was shipped or sampled to, and what came back. Part of it moved into another lot is followed there: whoever received that lot is listed too, its **Through lot** naming that lot, which opens its page. Under the lot’s code, a line says how many partners received it; press it to go to the list. On a phone each one is a card.

Some may have left with no recipient on record: shipped without an order, or sampled without a name. They are listed with no partner.

Beside **Who received it**, **Export** writes the recall list: the same rows, in your language, each lot’s supplier’s lot beside its code, with how much was shipped, sampled and returned and the day it first left. Its headings and words are in your language, the day is written year-month-day, and quantities are written plainly, 600 and not 600.0000. On the page, an expiry already past says how long ago: “Expired 4 days ago”.

> Only someone who may see costs sees the lot’s cost and how its value changed.

## Words you’ll meet {#words}

- **Lot code or supplier’s lot**: the code printed on the box or given here, or the supplier’s own lot kept beside a code made here. Either finds the lot.
- **Steps away**: how many runs lie between this lot and the other.
- **Not available**: a location whose stock is not for sending, such as a bin of returns waiting to be checked.
- **Batch**: what a run makes. It is a lot like any received one.
